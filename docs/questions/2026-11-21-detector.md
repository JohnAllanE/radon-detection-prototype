## Question 1: the ions land where the four dies meet

![Top view of the 2 by 2 array in the 21 mm bore. Four 5.67 mm dies with 5 mm active squares, separated by 0.5 mm gaps. A shaded cross 1.17 mm wide marks where the dies meet: the gaps plus each die's 0.335 mm inactive border. Two dashed circles, 1 mm and 3.5 mm in radius, enclose half and 80 percent of the ions that S-01 lands on the array.](diagrams/array-landing.svg)

**The finding.** S-01 shows 95–99 % of the ions formed in the gas reaching the array, which is good. But far from the board the field is weak, and its small radial part keeps pushing ions toward the tube's axis for the whole of their trip. Ions from the upper half of the chamber therefore land within a millimetre or two of the centre. That centre is exactly where the four dies meet. About two-thirds of the collected ions land on the 0.5 mm gaps or the dies' inactive borders. An alpha from an atom there has a poorer chance of being counted, or is counted with part of its energy.

**What this does and doesn't change.** Both boards in a chamber see the same pattern, so your comparison of premium and low-cost boards (R-53) is unaffected. The cost is count rate: about 16 % of alphas counted, against about 34 % if the ions spread evenly over the array (100 of its 140 mm² is active), before neutralisation losses. The estimate averages a round model around the axis; a 3D model would firm it up.

**Options.**

| Option | What changes | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| (a) Accept it now | Nothing | About half the counts an even spread would give; the RAD7 transfer calibrates the efficiency anyway |
| (b) A small positive electrode at the centre, under the gaps, to push ions sideways onto the dies | Detector board copper, one more simulation (3D) in pass 2 | They would then land mostly on inactive borders: small gain, `VERIFY` by simulation |
| (c) One die centred on the axis, the others around it | Breaks R-06's 2 × 2 array and the 76 mm comparison geometry | Layout redesign; three or five dies, not four |

**Recommendation:** (a) now, and a 3D field check of (b) in pass 2. The first runs' count rate, against the RAD7, will show how much it matters.

## Question 2: the premium board (D-18), and the ADA4817-1 test

![Horizontal bars of the electronic peak width at 4 microseconds shaping: OPA140 6.9 to 7.4 keV, LMP7721 6.8 to 7.3, OPA656 6.0 to 6.5, ADA4817-1 4.0 to 4.7, OPA657 5.1 to 5.7, all far left of the 100 keV resolution target drawn as a dashed line.](diagrams/premium-noise.svg)

**The finding.** Every candidate's electronic noise is 4–7 keV, and widths add in quadrature: $\sqrt{100^2 - 7^2} = 99.8$ keV, so the electronics take almost nothing of R-09's 100 keV. The detector itself (dead layer, entry angle) sets the peak width. Even the ADA4817-1, the quietest, would not show in the spectrum. D-16 (both ADCs) and D-17 (the low-cost footprint) are your own choices and stand; only D-18, the premium op-amp, needs your word.

**A datasheet point that changes D-18.** The LMP7721 runs from 1.8 to 5.5 V in total (LMP7721.json p5), and the detector board's CSAs run on ±4.5 V (9 V). The premium board would need its own ±2.5 V pair, two small LDOs from the ±4.5 V on the shared connector. A 8.78 MeV pulse is 389 mV plus up to 200 mV of dark-current offset, so ±2.5 V is enough swing.

| Option | Cost per channel | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| (a) LMP7721 with ±2.5 V LDOs on the premium board | Op-amp about $8.40 (your figure), plus two LDOs per board | Shows the leakage and guarding difference, not noise |
| (b) Premium = OPA140 with full guarding and layout care | Same part as the low-cost board | Cheapest; the difference is only layout |
| (c) ADA4817-1 on one channel | Price unknown (`VERIFY`), about 0.7 W per board heats the diodes | 4 against 7 keV: invisible against about 100 keV |

**Recommendation:** (a), fitted on all four premium channels if the priced BOM stays under R-74's CAD 20 per channel, otherwise on 1–2 as you said. No ADA4817-1 test now: it costs money and heat to show a difference the spectrum can't.

## Question 3: a series resistor of at least 10 MΩ between the HV board and the tube (A-21)

![The HV board's output feeds a 100 megohm over 100 kilohm divider, whose 1 volt per kilovolt tap goes to the feedback, an MCU ADC and a test point. After the divider, a series resistor of at least 10 megohms feeds the chamber tube, which carries only picoamps of ion current and has a small capacitance to ground. A dashed path marks a flashover from the tube to a diode or probe, limited to 100 microamps.](diagrams/hv-series.svg)

**The finding.** The electrometer asked for it (its A-18): if the tube flashes over to a diode or to the electrometer's probe, the resistor limits the current to 1 kV / 10 MΩ = 100 µA. The tube draws only picoamps of ion current, so the resistor drops nothing in use. With the tube's capacitance it also forms a low-pass filter on the HV ripple, which helps R-19 (the value awaits the capacitance, `VERIFY`).

**What it costs.** A resistor string rated for 1 kV: during a flashover the full voltage sits across it. Ten 1 MΩ chips in series (100 V each), or one HV resistor, on the 5.0 mm spacing rule. The readback divider must sit on the HV board's side of it, or it would read 10 % low ([HV spacing note](../notes/hv-spacing-and-readback.md)).

**Recommendation:** yes, 10 MΩ, after the divider.

## Question 4: what to record about the lab (Q-02)

![Four sources feed one run record kept in the host log: the MCU board gives the time, HV readback and PD bias; the operator gives air or nitrogen, flow or static and the valve state; room sensors give temperature, humidity and pressure at start and end; the boards give serials and the channels fitted.](diagrams/run-record.svg)

**Why it matters.** Nothing compensates for the environment (D-06, R-17), but the readings still depend on it: room radon follows ventilation and pressure, diode dark current rises steeply with temperature (for silicon, roughly doubling every 8–10 °C, `VERIFY` for the VTH2110), and the VTH2110 is rated only to 60 % RH (VTH2110.json p2). With these few numbers per run, a later look at the data can tell a drift from a real change.

**Proposed per run:** start and end time; room temperature and RH, start and end (a cheap hygrometer); barometric pressure, from a local weather service if no barometer; air or N₂, flow or static, flow rate; HV readback and PD bias; board serials and channels fitted. Only the first four need anything bought: one hygrometer.

**Recommendation:** adopt this list as R-27's content, recorded by hand or in the host log. No sensor on the MCU board (D-06 stands).

## Question 5: desiccant and filters, low cost first (Q-17)

![The Part 1 gas train: sample, filter, desiccant, filter, chamber with two ball valves, filter, pump. The three filters and the desiccant are outlined as the parts to source.](diagrams/gas-sources.svg)

**Context.** D-25 proposed one filter type at every port: a hydrophobic PTFE membrane, about 0.2 µm, with a stainless or PFA body. Those bodies are the costly part. R-30 only excludes silicone, rubber and PVC.

| Option | Cost | Limits |
|---|---|---|
| (a) Disposable 25 mm PTFE-membrane syringe filters, polypropylene housing, Luer fittings to the PTFE tubing | Low (price `VERIFY`) | Reverse-flow support and vacuum rating unstated: check for Part 2's vent-back |
| (b) Stainless in-line housings with PTFE membranes (D-25 as written) | Higher than (a), `VERIFY` | None for this use |
| Desiccant: indicating Drierite (D-13) in a refillable clear column | Low | The column's seals must avoid rubber (R-30): PTFE or PP fittings |

**Recommendation:** (a) for Part 1 now, polypropylene allowed by amending D-25; (b) only for Part 2 if a syringe filter can't take venting. Indicating Drierite in a clear refillable column.

## Question 6: the board outline and O-ring land (Q-20), with S-01's numbers

![A to-scale section of the tube end on the detector board. The dies reach 8.37 mm from the axis, the ring copper on +20 volts spans 8.67 to 9.6 mm, and the tube's inner wall is at 10.5 mm. The tube end, its edges broken to 0.5 mm radius, sits on an O-ring on bare laminate, 1 to 2 mm above the board.](diagrams/oring-land.svg)

**What S-01 fixes.** The O-ring sits on bare laminate with no copper under it; the tube end stands 1–2 mm above the board (2 mm lowers the peak field from about 1.5 to 1.0 kV/mm); both tube-end edges are broken to about 0.5 mm radius; the ring copper stops at 9.6 mm. The board must be at least the tube's 26.67 mm outer diameter plus whatever clamps it, which also settles the electrometer's "≤ 20 mm" quote: the back has more room than that.

**What only you can decide:** the O-ring size and groove (in the tube end or a clamp ring), and how the board is clamped (bolts through the board outside the O-ring, or a clamp ring). Both are machining choices.

**Recommendation:** a face-seal O-ring in a groove in a clamp flange on the tube end, the board clamped between that flange and a backing plate; O-ring size from a standard series that fits outside a 22 mm bore (`VERIFY`).

## Settled without you

- **OPA657 as a third tier (review question 3): no.** Its minimum stable gain is about 7 (OPA657.json, description and application note). With the test port's switch open, the CSA must be stable at a noise gain of 1 (R-60, electrometer R-19): the OPA657 would oscillate. OPA656 (unity-gain stable, 6.0–6.5 keV) remains a swap candidate on the low-cost footprint.
- **Die-to-wall gap and ring sign (review question 6, Q-24):** S-01, decisions 2026-11-21.
- **Not needed now:** converting R-xx to F/O/C (review question 8) changes no design: decide at the end of pass 2. Q-26 (bond or epoxy the dies) and Q-15 (suppliers): `DECIDE AT` parts and assembly. Q-11 (which face is the anode) is a measurement: a DMM diode test on a spare die, before layout.
